> 【供需法则完整定义补全】"Demand refers to how much (quantity) of a product or service is desired by buyers. The quantity demanded is the amount of a product people are willing to buy at a certain price; the relationship between price and quantity demanded is known as the demand relationship. The analyst studies the relationship between supply vs. demand using price and volume over time. Supply represents how much the market can offer. The quantity supplied refers to the amount of a certain good producers are willing to supply when receiving a certain price. The correlation between price and how much of a good or service is supplied to the market is known as the supply relationship. Price, therefore, is a reflection of supply and demand."

> 【因果法则完整定义补全】"A second basic principle underlying all analytical efforts is the law of cause and effect. The idea here is that in order for there to be an effect that shows up as a change in the price of a stock, there must first be a cause. In its most basic state, this law seems very much the same as the law of supply and demand. In the cases of the individual trades mentioned, the cause is the buyer's desire to hold the shares, or the seller's desire to have dollars. In one case the cause is expressed in terms of demand and in the other in terms of supply."

> "A cause can be stated in terms of the reason behind an individual trade. In the making of important profits in the stock market, however, the significance of each individual trade is greatly reduced. Here the idea of a cause must be taken more broadly. The effect realized by a cause will be in direct proportion to that cause. Consequently, to get an important move, or effect there must be an important cause. These are not built from one trade, but rather take time, sometimes a long time, to develop."

> "Generally these causes are built during an important shift in who is holding the stock. The flow of shares that is of greatest significance is the one that occurs as shares leave the strong hands of the professional traders and go to the weaker hands of the general public."

> "Every market advance begins only after the professional traders have all, or just about all, the shares they desire. Once the move begins, it will be carried forward primarily by the increasing and emotional buying of the public. The emotion at work here, by the way, is greed. The knowledgeable trader will go with the upward trend of the advance as long as prices continue to move up easily."

> "The idea is to measure this cause and project the extent of its effect. The excesses that develop in supply and demand are not random but are the result of key events in market action or the result of periods of preparation. This law's operation can be seen working as the force of accumulation or distribution within a trading range that works itself out in the subsequent move out of that trading range. This law can be seen working over a group of bars."

# CORE_CONCEPTS

1. **Law of Supply and Demand**: The foundational principle. Price reflects the imbalance between supply and demand. Analyst studies price and volume over time. Accumulation = demand gradually overcoming supply. Distribution = supply overcomes demand. The law of demand is inverse (lower price → more demand); the law of supply is direct (higher price → more supply). Supply and demand is the effect, not the cause — the "something" that changes supply/demand is the true cause.
2. **Law of Cause and Effect**: The effect (price move) is proportional to the cause (accumulation/distribution within a trading range). Causes take time to develop, sometimes a long time. Causes are built during an important shift in who is holding the stock (from strong/professional hands to weak/public hands, and vice versa). The idea is to measure this cause and project the extent of its effect.
3. **Law of Effort vs. Result**: Effort = volume. Result = price change (spread). Harmony promotes further movement; lack of harmony (divergence) promotes a change in direction. Can be observed on a single bar or over multiple bars. A classic four-day example: price advances +6, +4, +2, +0.5 points while volume expands 10k → 20k → 40k → 100k shares — a clear case of effort without corresponding result, warning of potential trouble.
4. **Accumulation Schematic Phases (A-E)**:
   - Phase A: Stop the downward trend (permanently or temporarily). Key events: PS (1), SC (2), AR (3).
   - Phase B: Build the cause within the trading range. Key events: ST (4, 5, 8), Creek (6).
   - Phase C: Smart money tests the market. Key events: Springs/Shakeouts (7), and/or Jumps and Backups.
   - Phase D: Defines the "line of least resistance" with the passage of the nine buying tests. Key events: Jump (9), SOS (10, 12), BU/LPS (13).
   - Phase E: The markup or upward trending phase unfolds.
5. **Accumulation Key Structural Events** (numbered by position in schematic):
   - **PS (1) Preliminary Support**: Substantial buying after prolonged down-move; volume and spread widen; signal that down move may be ending.
   - **SC (2) Selling Climax**: Widening spread and selling pressure climax; public panicky selling absorbed by professionals near bottom; defines lower level of TR.
   - **AR (3) Automatic Rally**: Selling pressure exhausted; wave of buying pushes up prices, fueled by short covering.
   - **ST (4, 5, 8) Secondary Test**: Price revisits SC area to test supply/demand; if bottom confirmed, significant supply should not resurface, volume and spread diminished.
   - **Creek (6)**: Wavy line of resistance across rally peaks within TR; minor and significant supply lines that must be crossed before onward movement.
   - **Spring/Shakeout (7)**: Occurs late in TR; dominant players test available supply. Light volume on break = way clear for advance. Heavy supply = renewed decline. Moderate volume = more testing needed. Also provides dominant interests with additional supply from weak holders at low prices.
   - **Jump (9)**: Price jumps through the resistance line (Creek); bullish if achieved with increasing speed and volume.
   - **SOS (10, 12) Sign of Strength**: Advance on increasing spread and volume, usually over some resistance level.
   - **BU/LPS (13) Backing Up / Last Point of Support**: Pullback to support that was formerly resistance, on diminished spread and volume after an SOS. Good place to initiate long positions or add to profitable ones.
6. **Distribution Schematic Phases (A-E)**: Mirror of accumulation but inverted.
   - Phase A: Stop upward trend. Key events: PS (1) Preliminary Supply, BC (2) Buying Climax, AR (3) Automatic Reaction.
   - Phase B: Build cause for downward move. Key events: ST (Secondary Test), SOW (10), Ice line.
   - Phase C: Test upper boundaries. Key events: Upthrust, UTAD.
   - Phase D: Line of least resistance downward. Key events: LPSY.
   - Phase E: Markdown or downward trending phase unfolds.
7. **Distribution Key Structural Events**:
   - **PS (1) Preliminary Supply**: Substantial selling after up move; volume and spread widen; signal up move may be ending.
   - **BC (2) Buying Climax**: Widening spread and buying force climax; public urgent buying filled by professionals near top.
   - **AR (3) Automatic Reaction**: Buying exhausted; heavy supply continues; low of selloff defines bottom of TR.
   - **ST Secondary Test**: Revisits BC area; if top confirmed, supply outweighs demand, volume and spread diminished near BC resistance.
   - **SOW (10) Sign of Weakness**: Occurs on increased spread and volume compared to rally to point 9; supply showing dominance.
   - **Ice**: Wavy line of support under reaction lows of TR. Break through followed by attempts to get back above. "Fall on the ice" metaphor — first fall holds, then attempt to forge ahead fails.
   - **Upthrust**: Opposite of spring; price moves above resistance of TR and quickly reverses back into TR. A "bull trap" — appears to signal uptrend start but marks end of up move.
   - **UTAD Upthrust After Distribution**: Similar to spring/terminal shakeout in accumulation; occurs in latter stages of distribution TR. Definitive test of new demand after breakout above TR resistance. Light volume with no follow-through, or heavy volume with breakdown back into TR center = evidence of distribution, not accumulation. Weak short holders give up to dominant interests; more distribution to new uninformed buyers.
   - **LPSY (Last Point of Supply)**: After testing ice (support) on SOW, feeble rally on narrow spread shows difficulty making further rise. Volume may be light (weak demand) or heavy (substantial supply). Last waves of distribution unloaded before markdown. After ice break, rally thwarted at ice's surface (now resistance). Good place to initiate short positions or add to profitable ones.
8. **The Nine Buying Tests**: A checklist framework used in Phase D to confirm entry. Mentioned but not enumerated in this 14-page deck. Requires a full Wyckoff reference for exact criteria.
9. **Reward/Risk Framework**: One objective of Wyckoff is to improve market timing when establishing a speculative position in anticipation of a coming move where a favorable reward/risk ratio exists to justify taking that position.
10. **Professional vs. Public**: The core narrative is tracking when shares move from strong hands (professional traders) to weak hands (public), and vice versa. Every market advance begins after professionals have accumulated shares; the move is then carried by emotional public buying (greed). Distribution eliminates long positions and often involves establishing speculative shorts.

# FORMULAS_AND_ALGOS

- **Effort-Result Divergence (single bar)**:
  - Effort = Volume_t
  - Result = Range_t = High_t - Low_t (or |Close_t - Open_t|)
  - Flag divergence if Result decreases while Effort increases across consecutive bars, or vice versa.
- **Effort-Result Divergence (multi-bar, 4-day benchmark)**:
  - Track N consecutive bars
  - Price_momentum = sum(Close_t - Close_{t-1}) for t=1..N
  - Volume_momentum = sum(Volume_t) for t=1..N
  - Divergence = (Price_momentum decreasing) AND (Volume_momentum increasing)
  - Example benchmark: N=4, Price_momentum = [6, 4, 2, 0.5] pts, Volume_momentum = [10k, 20k, 40k, 100k] shares
- **Cause Proxy (TR magnitude)**:
  - Cause = (TR_high - TR_low) × (number of bars in TR)
  - This is a proxy for the "cause" that should be proportional to the subsequent "effect" (move out of TR).
- **Reward/Risk Ratio (structural)**:
  - Entry = breakout price from TR (after Phase D confirmation, at Jump or SOS)
  - Stop = LPS level (accumulation) or Ice break level (distribution), or TR support boundary
  - Target = Entry + (TR_high - TR_low) (projecting the width of the TR)
  - RRR = (Target - Entry) / (Entry - Stop)
- **Volume Confirmation Filter (Jump / SOS / SOW)**:
  - Breakout_valid = (Volume_breakout > SMA(Volume, 20)) AND (Range_breakout > SMA(Range, 20))
- **Spring Volume Assessment (three-tier)**:
  - Light_volume = Volume_break < 0.5 × SMA(Volume, 20) → Way clear for sustained advance (bullish)
  - Heavy_volume = Volume_break > 1.5 × SMA(Volume, 20) → Renewed decline (bearish)
  - Moderate_volume = 0.5 × SMA < Volume_break < 1.5 × SMA → More testing needed (neutral/caution)
- **Creek / Ice Line Proxy**:
  - Creek = Resistance line drawn across rally peaks within TR (max of local highs)
  - Ice = Support line drawn under reaction lows within TR (min of local lows)
  - Break_Creek = Close > Creek AND Volume > SMA(Volume, 20) AND Range > SMA(Range, 20)
  - Break_Ice = Close < Ice AND (Volume > SMA(Volume, 20) OR subsequent failed retest)

# NOT_QUANT_YET

2. **Phase Transition Thresholds**: The text describes phases qualitatively (e.g., "Phase A: to stop a downward trend") without numerical thresholds for when one phase ends and another begins. The schematic is a visual framework, not a time-series algorithm.
3. **Visual Schematic Pattern Recognition**: The core of Wyckoff is reading structural patterns (springs, upthrusts, SOS, SOW, creek, ice) that are visually identified on charts. This requires either human judgment or advanced pattern-recognition ML trained on labeled Wyckoff schematics.
4. **"Dominant Players" vs. "Public" Tracking**: The narrative about strong hands vs. weak hands is conceptual. No direct proxy for tracking professional vs. retail order flow is provided in this text. Would require Level 2/tick data or order-flow imbalance metrics.

7. **LPS and LPSY Diminished Volume/Spread**: "Diminished spread and volume" and "narrow spread" are relative terms without specific thresholds (e.g., <50% of breakout bar, <20th percentile). Requires calibration per instrument and timeframe.
